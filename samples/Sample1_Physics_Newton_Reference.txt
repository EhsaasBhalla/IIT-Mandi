Newton's Laws of Motion - Additional Reference Material

1. Historical Context:
Isaac Newton published these laws in 1687 in his seminal work "Philosophiæ Naturalis Principia Mathematica". Before Newton, Aristotle's view (that a continuous force is required to keep an object moving) dominated physics for nearly 2,000 years.

2. Real-World Applications for Students:
- Seatbelts (1st Law): When a car crashes, the car stops but the passengers keep moving forward due to inertia. Seatbelts provide the external force to stop them.
- Rockets (3rd Law): A rocket engine pushes exhaust gases downwards (action), and the gases push the rocket upwards (reaction).

3. Common Misconceptions to Address:
- Students often think that if an object is moving, there must be a force pushing it. (Clarify inertia).
- Students confuse mass and weight. (Mass is constant, weight depends on gravity).
