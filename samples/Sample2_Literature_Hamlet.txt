# English Literature: Understanding Shakespeare's Hamlet

## Grade Level: 12th Grade
## Subject: English Literature

### Overview
This unit focuses on William Shakespeare's tragedy, "Hamlet, Prince of Denmark." We will explore themes of madness, revenge, corruption, and the complexity of human action versus inaction.

### Core Themes

1. **The Hesitation of Action**
Unlike traditional revenge tragedies where the protagonist acts swiftly, Hamlet is defined by his philosophical hesitation. His famous "To be, or not to be" soliloquy encapsulates his internal debate about life, death, and the morality of taking action against his uncle, Claudius.

2. **Madness: Feigned vs. Real**
Hamlet claims he will put on an "antic disposition" (fake madness) to investigate his father's murder. However, as the play progresses, the line between his feigned madness and genuine psychological breakdown becomes blurred, especially in his interactions with Ophelia.

3. **Corruption and Decay**
The physical and moral decay of Denmark is a central motif. "Something is rotten in the state of Denmark," says Marcellus. The poisoning of the king mirrors the political and moral poisoning of the kingdom.

### Learning Objectives
- Students will be able to analyze the psychological depth of the protagonist.
- Students will identify and interpret soliloquies and their dramatic function.
- Students will debate whether Hamlet's madness is an act or a reality.

### Key Characters
- **Hamlet**: The Prince of Denmark, melancholic and philosophical.
- **Claudius**: The antagonist, Hamlet's uncle, the current King who murdered his brother.
- **Ophelia**: Polonius's daughter, a tragic figure driven to genuine madness by the surrounding corruption.

### Discussion Prompt
If Hamlet had killed Claudius immediately in the chapel when he had the chance, would the play still be a tragedy? Why did Shakespeare force Hamlet to wait?
